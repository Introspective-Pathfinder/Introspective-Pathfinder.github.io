Here's a simple warm-up problem to kick things off.

**Problem:** Show that for any integer $n$, the number $n^3 - n$ is
always divisible by 6.

$$
n^3 - n = n(n-1)(n+1)
$$

Since these are three consecutive integers, one of them is divisible
by 3, and at least one is divisible by 2. So the product is divisible
by $2 \times 3 = 6$.
